04 / A LOOK BACK

I didn't have a
team conflict.
I had a timing problem.

Our interdisciplinary team worked well together. Coordinating seven people's commitments near overlapping deadlines was still difficult, particularly when many of us were working alongside university.

BEFORETHE PRESSURE POINTWHAT I DID DIFFERENTLY
01

I waited to contribute.

At first, I was comfortable completing my assigned portion and responding when someone needed something. That worked for my chapter, but it did not always help me see the dependencies between sections.

02

Deadlines converged.

The final introduction, conclusion and integration needed more coordination than our independent chapters. Different work schedules meant we could not assume everyone would be available at the same time.

03

I became more proactive.

I provided earlier updates about my HR section, asked for input, contributed feedback and helped clarify who was handling particular work. We also relied on scheduled Zoom meetings and Teams updates to maintain a shared view of progress.

THE RECORD / TEAM 4K

Evidence over adjectives.

Our Teams discussions document work distribution, agreed deadlines and meeting arrangements. Rather than claim that communication was simply a strength, I can point to specific routines that made coordination more reliable.

The extracts are intentionally softened to protect classmates' identities on this public site. My submitted material and source screenshots remain the original evidence.

WHAT I CAN ACTUALLY CLAIM

Communication helped.
It wasn't a complete solution.

Scheduled calls and regular messages made it easier for us to check progress and identify gaps. But we still worked close to some deadlines. If I approached the task again, I would establish earlier check-in dates and use a shared responsibilities tracker with named owners and intermediate milestones.

That would give us a better way to assess whether a change worked: fewer unresolved handovers, earlier identification of delays and clearer ownership of the work.

The HR Business Partner at Canva described a situation where a technically capable colleague struggled to bring others into the process. That example challenged my assumption that completing my own component well was enough. How I communicate the work also affects whether others can use it.

THE MOST USEFUL LESSON

Being responsible for my part of a project is only the beginning. The harder skill is noticing where my part connects to everyone else's.
