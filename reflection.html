03 / Reflection & evidence

From showing up
to stepping in.

I learned that contributing to a group isn't only about finishing my section. It's also about making it easier for everyone to move forward.

The real challenge

Seven people. Different schedules. One deadline.

Our Capstone group generally worked well together; conflict wasn't the main issue. The harder part was coordinating students with different jobs, timetables and responsibilities while assessment deadlines approached.

Our initial agility exercise added further pressure. The team had one week to complete its deliverable. According to our structured debrief, an additional member joined after roles had been allocated and an unfamiliar accounting component was introduced. The team identified ambiguity and control as major sources of stress.

Change in behaviour

I became more proactive.

Before

I often focused mainly on my allocated responsibilities and contributed when asked.

During

With deadlines approaching, I gave more feedback on teammates' work, helped delegate tasks and contributed to regular updates. We used scheduled Zoom meetings alongside Teams messages to keep shared progress visible.

After

Regular communication and clear completion times helped the group coordinate its contributions and meet deadlines. I came to see accountability as a shared process rather than just an individual obligation.

“Time management isn't only about my calendar. It's also about understanding who is waiting on my work.”
What the evidence does—and doesn't—show

What worked

The team debrief records open communication, advance planning, clear roles and internal due dates as contributors to success. It also notes that knowing which tasks were complete helped reduce stress.

What I would improve

A positive self-rating doesn't prove that every aspect of our process was ideal. Next time I'd establish dependencies and milestones earlier, document owners more consistently and ask whether updates are actually useful—not simply frequent.

Transfer to professional work

Different setting, familiar pressure.

At the same time, I was navigating full-time paralegal work and full-time university study. Managing correspondence, records and competing deadlines at work made the Capstone's coordination challenges particularly relevant. Both experiences reinforced the need to provide timely updates, seek clarification and consider how my work affects others.
