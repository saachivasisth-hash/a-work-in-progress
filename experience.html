02 / EMPLOYMENT ARCHIVE

Three places.
Different lessons.

A career chronology that begins with tutoring, moves through fast-paced hospitality and now sits in legal practice.
2021 TO 2022
EDUCATION / KUMON

Learning isn't one-size-fits-all.

I taught children, recorded their progress, maintained learning records and updated my supervisor about when individual students were ready to move levels or needed additional support. I also spoke with parents and adapted my approach to different learning needs.

THE CHALLENGE

Starting work while still at school, and learning how to discuss a student's difficulties with parents when I was a student myself.

WHAT CHANGED

Supporting students made me more reflective about my own HSC learning. I began paying closer attention to areas needing practice rather than expecting the same pace of progress from everyone.

2023 TO 2025
HOSPITALITY / GUZMAN Y GOMEZ

Learning to keep up.

As a crew member, I managed orders, payments, telephone enquiries, drive-through service and customer concerns. During busy shifts, I helped allocate tasks, communicated updates and kept track of competing priorities.

THE CHALLENGE

Finding work after school, becoming comfortable in a customer-facing role and keeping pace when priorities changed without warning.

WHAT CHANGED

I developed a habit of mentally ordering tasks by urgency. I also learned that working independently and asking for help are not opposites. Clear updates and shared responsibilities are often what make independent work possible.

2026 TO NOW
LEGAL PRACTICE / LEGAL ASSISTANT

Where details matter.

I work in a personal injury practice. My work includes drafting letters of advice, responding to client enquiries, diarising, organising bookings, preparing files and supporting administrative workflows.

THE CHALLENGE

Moving into a professional legal environment while working and studying full-time. Deadlines carry a different weight when a delay may affect a client's claim.

WHAT CHANGED

I'm more deliberate about records, professional judgement and keeping people informed. The lesson I brought from hospitality is to ask questions early, rather than let uncertainty become someone else's problem.

THE CONNECTION

Across these roles, I've become more aware that good work is rarely just about finishing what is in front of you. It also depends on how well you read the situation around you, how clearly you communicate, and whether the people you work with have what they need to keep moving.
