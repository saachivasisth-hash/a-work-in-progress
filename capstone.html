The workforce question was never just about hiring.

Our group proposed sustainable development opportunities at Hillview Estate. My HR chapter considered how the project could create local employment and training while respecting the commercial realities of a developing vineyard and tourism operation.

I worked across Finance, Economics and Marketing assumptions. In particular, I had to balance immediate visitor-service staffing against the longer-term need for specialist viticultural knowledge.

EXHIBIT A / ORIGINAL COURSEWORK

A decision with trade-offs.

I created a staffing comparison to assess different options based on cost, flexibility, consistent support and opportunities for employee development. This helped me recommend sharing a specialist Viticulturist during the early stages of the project, while ensuring trainees received proper supervision and important knowledge was passed on.

EXHIBIT B / ROLE DESIGN

Turning workforce needs into a role.

My Viticulturist role specification connected technical work with the responsibility to train and mentor seasonal employees. The point was to design a position that would build capability, not only fill a vacancy.

THE PROPOSAL

Access matters. So does progression.

The Harvest Pathways Program and Green Skills Academy aimed to create paid opportunities for people entering the workforce and provide practical training in sustainable skills, supporting SDG 8. I also recognised that offering jobs or training alone was not enough. Real success would depend on whether participants developed useful skills and gained opportunities for future employment.

FROM INDUSTRY TO MY WORK

Two ideas I carried into the project.

DIRECTOR OF DESIGN AND EDUCATION, THE KING'S FOUNDATION AUSTRALIA

His discussion of how commercial activities can support education helped me understand the importance of balancing financial needs with opportunities for learning. I applied this to Hillview by considering how seasonal employment could provide fair working conditions and practical training while supporting the project's financial sustainability.

LEAD AI CONSULTANT, ADAPTIVE

Her discussion of introducing new technology made me realise that giving employees access to tools is not enough. They also need the training, support and confidence to use them effectively. This influenced my approach to the Green Skills Academy, where I emphasised practical learning and assessing employees' skills rather than relying on technology alone.
