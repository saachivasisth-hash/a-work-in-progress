The workforce question was never just about hiring.

Our group proposed sustainable development opportunities at Hillview Estate. My HR chapter considered how the project could create local employment and training while respecting the commercial realities of a developing vineyard and tourism operation.

I worked across Finance, Economics and Marketing assumptions. In particular, I had to balance immediate visitor-service staffing against the longer-term need for specialist viticultural knowledge.

EXHIBIT A / ORIGINAL COURSEWORK

A decision with trade-offs.

I developed a staffing comparison that weighed flexibility, continuity, staff development and coverage. It supported using shared viticultural expertise during establishment, with safeguards around supervision and knowledge transfer.

EXHIBIT B / ROLE DESIGN

Turning workforce needs into a role.

My Viticulturist role specification connected technical work with the responsibility to train and mentor seasonal employees. The point was to design a position that would build capability, not only fill a vacancy.

THE PROPOSAL

Access matters. So does progression.

The Harvest Pathways Program and Green Skills Academy proposed supervised, paid entry-level pathways and skills training linked to SDG 8. My chapter also recognised their limits: recruitment volumes are not evidence of meaningful development unless participants gain transferable competencies.
